Types of finance papers
Academic Papers Come in Types
Each type has a different purpose and structure
Knowing the type = knowing what to look for
Main Types

Type 1 – Theory Paper
Goal: Develop a new model or theory
Core: Abstract reasoning + mathematical proof
Structure:
1.
Introduction (motivation + question)
2.
The Model (assumptions + equations)
3.
Proofs / Calibration
4.
Conclusion (implications + testable predictions)
Example: Black-Scholes option pricing model
How to read: Focus on assumptions + implications. Don't fear the math.
Type 2 – Empirical Paper

Goal: Test a hypothesis with data

Core: Evidence + statistical analysis

Structure:
1.
Introduction (question)
2.
Data & Summary Statistics
3.
Methodology (regression model)
4.
Results (tables + findings)
5.
Robustness Checks
6.
Conclusion

Example: "Does gender diversity on boards affect firm performance?"

How to read: Focus on Data, Methodology, Results. Look at the tables!
Type 2b – Empirical with a Small Model
Goal: Use simple theory to guide empirical testing
Core: Model motivates the hypothesis
Structure:
1.
Conceptual Framework / Simple Model (short section)
2.
Makes a clear, testable prediction
3.
Then standard empirical sections (Data, Results, etc.)
Example: Model shows why CEOs might prefer stock compensation → test if 
firms with more stock compensation perform better
How to read: Understand the model's prediction. Then read it like an 
empirical paper.
Type 4 – Experimental Paper

Goal: Study behavior in controlled settings

Core: Lab/field design + human subjects

Structure:
1.
Introduction
2.
Theory / Hypotheses
3.
Experimental Design (treatments, variables)
4.
Procedures (subjects, incentives)
5.
Results
6.
Discussion + Conclusion

Example: Testing if traders form bubbles in simulated asset markets

How to read: Focus on Design, Treatments, Incentives. Are subjects properly 
motivated?
Type 5 – Methodological Paper
Goal: Introduce, compare, or validate research methods
Core: Focus on how research should be done, not new findings
Structure:
1.
Introduction (what problem with existing methods?)
2.
Description of new method / technique
3.
Validation (simulations or applications)
4.
Comparison with existing approaches
5.
Conclusion + recommendations
Example: "A New Approach to Testing for Market Efficiency Using Machine 
Learning”
How to read: Focus on what problem it solves and whether you could apply 
this method in your own work
Type 5 – Review Paper
Goal: Summarize and synthesize existing research
Core: No new results – but a roadmap of the field
Structure:
1.
Introduction (scope)
2.
Thematic sections (organizes literature)
3.
Discussion + Future Research
4.
Comprehensive references
Example: "Thirty Years of Prospect Theory in Finance: A Review"
How to read: Your best friend when starting a new topic! Read for big 
picture + key papers.

The Three-Pass Method
Don't Read Like a Novel!
Most students: start at page 1, read to end → inefficient
Better: Three passes, each with a clear goal
Based on Keshav (2007) – "How to Read a Paper"
Pass 1 – The Bird's-Eye View
Pass 1 (5–10 minutes)
Goal: Get general idea + decide if you need to read further
Steps:
1.
Read title + abstract
▪
Think about the research design if you were the author
2.
Read introduction (especially last paragraph)
3.
Read section headings
4.
Read conclusion
5.
(Optional) Glance at references
At the end: You should answer the 5 Cs (next slide)
Cookson et al. (2026) - Abstract
The 5 Cs
After Pass 1, ask:
1.
Category: What type of paper? (Theory? Empirical? Review?)
2.
Context: What field? (Corporate finance? Asset pricing? )
3.
Correctness: Do assumptions seem reasonable?
4.
Contributions: What is the main claim?
5.
Clarity: Is it well-written?
Decision: Stop here (if not relevant) OR continue to Pass 2
Pass 2 – The Grasp

Pass 2 (About 1 hour)

Goal: Understand main argument + evidence (not every detail)

Steps:
1.
Read with more care
2.
Skip complex math / long proofs
3.
Focus on figures and tables – this is the evidence!
4.
Jot notes in margins
5.
Mark important references for later

At the end: Can you summarize the paper's main argument + evidence to a 
classmate?
Reading Tables Like a Pro
Tips for Regression Tables:
Look at main variables of interest
Check for stars (***, **, *) – statistical significance
Ask: Is the effect economically large?
Look at sample size (N) and R-squared
Tips for Graphs:
Are axes labeled?
Do they show error bars?
What story does the graph tell?

Pass 3 – The Deep Dive
Pass 3 (4–5 hours beginners, 1 hour experts)

Goal: Complete understanding – for papers you'll review, build on, or present

The key: "Virtually re-implement" the paper

Steps:
1.
Challenge every assumption
2.
Re-derive the math
3.
Re-create the empirical test in your mind
4.
Examine robustness checks

Ask "What's next?" – future research ideas

At the end: Can you reconstruct the entire paper from memory? Can you discuss 
strengths + weaknesses?

Keeping Records
Why Keep Records?
You will forget!
Future you (writing a dissertation) will thank you
Essential for literature reviews
Helps you connect ideas across papers
"The faintest pencil is better than the sharpest memory."

Option 2 – Reference Managers
Powerful Tolls: Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote
Benefits:
One-click import from web
Store + annotate PDFs
Searchable notes
Auto-generate bibliographies (huge time saver!)
Recommendation: Start with Zotero – free, easy, widely used.
Tip: download the paper and use “link to file”
Option 3 – Digital Notes
Advance: Notion, Obsidian, Roam Research
Benefits:
Create linked databases
Connect ideas across papers
Build a "second brain" for research
Best for: Students planning a thesis or long-term research

Final Tips
Remember:
Not every paper deserves a full Pass 3
Skim first, then dive deep only when needed
Build your reference library early
Connect papers – they talk to each other!
Review your notes, and reorganize if necessary!
For your literature review: Start with a recent review paper. Use its 
references as your reading list.
Reading papers is a skill. Practice makes perfect.
