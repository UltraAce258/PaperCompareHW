Part 1 – Writing the Body
Writing the Body
The body answers one central question:
Why should the reader believe your answer?
We Focus on three types of Finance papers:
Theoretical Paper
The mechanism follows logically from assumptions
Empirical Paper
The results are supported by credible data and identification
Experimental Paper
The treatment causes the observed outcome
Theoretical Paper Body Structure
1. Motivation and Economic Setting 
2. Model Assumptions 
3. Agents, Timing, and Payoffs 
4. Equilibrium / Solution Concept 
5. Main Propositions 
6. Economic Intuition 
7. Predictions / Implications 
The Role of a Model
A theoretical paper offers a structured explanation of a economic
mechanism/phenomenon of interest.
The model is a tool, not a reality.
Include the essential elements of the target problem 
Abstract away from unnecessary details 
Clarify a mechanism 
Generate results or predictions
Question: What is the minimum set of ingredients needed 
to explain investor overtrading?
Model Setup
A theory model should clearly define:
Who are the agents? 
What information do they have? 
What choices do they make? 
What are their objectives? 
What is the timing? 
What is the equilibrium concept? 
Assumptions: Order and Grouping
When writing assumptions:

Start with natural assumptions 

Move to stronger or more restrictive assumptions 

Group assumptions by category 

Use meaningful names, not only numbers 
Example categories:

Investor preferences 

Information structure 

Asset payoff process 

Market constraints
A1: The investor is overconfident.
A2: The return is normally distributed.
A3: The investor has CARA utility.
A4: Trading is costly.
A5: The investor observes a signal.
Preferences: The investor has CARA utility and 
may receive psychological utility from trading.
Information: The investor observes a noisy 
signal about future returns.
Asset returns: Risky asset returns are normally 
distributed.
Market friction: Trading involves a proportional 
transaction cost.
Notations
Good notation should be easy to guess.
Use meaningful symbols 
Define every variable 
Use consistent notation 
Avoid notation used only once 
“Let Ω denote the set of all possible psychological states.” But you never use Ω again.
“Investors may differ in their psychological benefit from trading.”
Avoid too many subscripts and superscripts if feasible
Example:
c𝑡= 𝑓𝑊𝑡
where 𝑐𝑡is consumption and 𝑊𝑡is wealth.
Definitions Need Examples
For a central property, show:
1.
An object that satisfies it 
2.
An object that does not satisfy it 
3.
A borderline case
Example:
An investor is “attention constrained” if she can 
process at most one signal per period.
1.
Investor observes only earnings news. 
2.
Investor processes all firm, industry, and 
macro signals.
3.
Investor can process two signals but ignores 
macro news.
Theorems and Propositions
Formal results should be:
Stated in simple English 
Written in parallel format 
Clearly linked to assumptions 
Followed by economic intuition
Testable implications
Proof or proof sketch is need:
Step 1, Step 2, Claim 1, Claim 2, Case 1/Case 2
Step 1: Show the objective function is concave.
Step 2: Derive the first-order condition.
Step 3: Compare the optimal trading level with the 
benchmark case.
Math to English in Proof: 52% - 63.5%
Proposition Template
A good theory result explains:
What the proposition says 
When it holds 
Why it holds 
What it implies
Template:
“Proposition 1 shows that when [condition], [result]. The 
intuition is that [mechanism]. This implies that we should 
observe [prediction].”
Example:
Proposition 1 shows that investors are less willing to realize 
losses when leverage is available. The intuition is that 
leverage allows investors to maintain risky positions 
beyond their mental budget, increasing reluctance to close 
losing positions. This implies that leverage may strengthen 
the disposition effect.

How do we know the estimated effect is credible?
Institutional Background: Not a
Textbook
What is the relevant setting? 
What institutional feature 
creates useful variation?
Why is this setting suitable for 
the research question? 
What does the reader need to 
know before seeing the 
empirical design?
Example 1:
China’s stock market has developed rapidly since the 1990s.
The staggered introduction of margin trading across eligible 
stocks creates variation in financing constraints and trading 
opportunities, allowing us to examine how access to margin 
trading affects stock liquidity.
Example 2:
China’s capital market is important and has developed 
rapidly in recent years. Many reforms have affected listed 
firms.
The reform was implemented in stages across firms, creating 
variation in treatment timing. This institutional feature 
allows us to compare affected and unaffected firms before 
and after the reform.

Data source 
Sample period
Unit of observation 
Sample restrictions 
Variable construction 
Final sample size
Example 1:

Example 2:
We use CSMAR data.
We obtain accounting variables from CSMAR, stock return 
data from RESSET, and M&A transaction data from Wind. 
We exclude financial firms, ST firms, and observations with 
missing key variables.”
Empirical Results
Do not just report significance.
A good empirical result explains:
Direction 
Magnitude 
Statistical significance 
Economic meaning 
Link to hypothesis 
Example 1:
Table 2 shows significant results. It is consistent with
hypothesis 1.
Example 2:
The 
results, 
presented 
in 
Table 
X, 
show 
that 
OverseaExperience is positively and significantly associated

that such executives play a critical role in driving cross-
border M&A activity.

Experimental Paper: Design Details
A good experiment section should explain:
Who are the participants? 
What is randomly assigned? 
What does the treatment change? 
What does the control group receive? 
What outcomes are measured? 
When are outcomes measured?
Design an experiment
Many young investors misunderstand investment risk. You want to 
test whether presenting risk information visually improves their 
portfolio choices. In particular, you compare a standard text 
warning with a visual risk chart.
Experimental Paper: Validity Concerns
Experiment papers must address:
Internal validity 
Was assignment random? 
Are groups balanced? 
Did participants comply? 
External validity 
Does the result generalize? 
Is the setting realistic? 
Mechanism 
Why did the treatment work?
Experiment Results
A good experiment result explains:
Treatment effect 
Control group benchmark 
Statistical significance 
Magnitude Mechanism or 
heterogeneity
Example 1:
The treatment group behaves differently.
Example 2:

percentage points more likely to choose diversified

Variable Definitions
For each key variable, define:
What it measures 
How it is constructed 
Whether it is continuous, 
binary, or categorical 
Expected direction 
Data source 
Key variables:
Dependent variable 
Main explanatory variable 
Control variables 
Fixed effects
Example:
We use Size, ROA, Leverage, and Cash as controls.
Firm size is measured as the natural logarithm of total assets. ROA is net income 
divided by total assets. Leverage is total liabilities divided by total assets. Cash is 
cash and cash equivalents scaled by total assets.

Mechanism Tests
Mechanism tests answer: Why
does this effect occur?
Proposed channel
Testable implication
Empirical test
Interpretation
E.g., financing constraints,
information asymmetry,
bargaining power, monitoring,
investor attention...
Exercise:
Research question: Does analyst coverage
affect stock liquidity?
Possible mechanism: Analyst coverage
reduces information asymmetry.

Heterogeneity vs Mechanism
Heterogeneity asks:
For whom is the effect stronger?
Mechanism asks:
Why does the effect occur?
Heterogeneity can support mechanism only when it is linked to theory.
Robustness Checks
Does the result survive alternative choices?
Alternative variable definitions 
Alternative samples 
Additional controls 
Placebo tests 
Pre-trend tests 
Alternative estimation methods 
A good robustness section is organized by concerns, not by tables.
Example:
To address the concern that our findings depend 
on the measure of risk-taking, we replace the risky 
asset share with the probability of holding any 
risky asset. The coefficient remains positive and 
statistically significant.
Exercise: Mechanism or Heterogeneity
Classify each statement as mechanism, heterogeneity, or neither.
1.
The effect is stronger for firms with high information asymmetry. 
2.
The coefficient remains significant after adding controls. 
3.
The effect is stronger for firms with limited analyst coverage, consistent with 
an information channel. 
4.
The result holds after excluding financial firms.

Organizing the Bibliography
Conclusion Structure
Your last chance to shape what readers remember.
(Typically about one page)
Restate the research question briefly 
Highlight the main finding 
Explain why the finding matters 
Suggest broader implications 
End with a clear final message
Abstract: What is this paper about?
Introduction: Why should we care?
Body: How do we know the answer?
Conclusion: What should we remember?
Bibliography: Who are we building on?
Conclusion by Paper Type
Empirical:
Main finding, credibility, economic implication
Theoretical:
Mechanism, new prediction, conceptual contribution
Experimenta
l:
Treatment effect, causal interpretation, external validity
Bibliography
Which academic conversation you enter 
Which ideas you build on 
Which methods you follow 
Where your evidence comes from 
Your academic credibility 
❖Empirical: Data sources, identification methods, related empirical findings
❖Theory: Foundational models, assumptions, solution concepts            
❖Experiment: Design papers, treatment literature, behavioral mechanisms
Citation Style and Integrity
Cite papers you actually read 
Cite ideas, not just names 
Keep in-text citations and bibliography consistent 
Use a consistent format 
Verify all references
Warning:
AI may generate fake references!
Example Reference Format
In text:
“Fama and French (1992) show that size and book-to-market explain cross-
sectional stock returns.”
Reference list:
Fama, Eugene F., and Kenneth R. French, 1992, The cross-section of expected 
stock returns, Journal of Finance 47, 427–465.
Reference Format
Order references alphabetically by the first author’s last name. 
If the same author has multiple papers, order them by year. 
If the same author has multiple papers in the same year, use 2020a, 2020b. 
Make sure every in-text citation appears in the reference list. 
Make sure every reference in the list is cited in the text. 
Use one citation style consistently. (Don’t mix APA、MLA、Chicago citation 
styles)
